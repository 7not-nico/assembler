**Procedure**

1. **Survey the text** — identify text type (narrative, argumentative, expository), structural markers, stated purpose, and scope. Note author and context.

2. **Extract literal content** — identify main ideas, supporting claims, factual assertions, and key terms. Distinguish major from minor points. Create a proposition inventory.

3. **Map structure** — trace relationships between parts: cause-effect, contrast, sequence, generalization-example. Identify transitions, topic shifts, and organizational pattern.

4. **Draw inferences** — connect stated information to derive implicit conclusions. Identify assumptions the author relies on. Use → for inference chains.

5. **Evaluate critically** — assess argument strength, evidence quality, author perspective, and potential biases. Distinguish fact from opinion. Identify rhetorical devices and their effect.

6. **Synthesize** — produce a concise summary with key claims, evidence, and reasoning. State conclusions anchored to specific textual evidence.

**Gotchas**

- Analysis progression follows order: literal extraction → structural mapping → inference → critical evaluation. Each step completes before the next. Evaluation after all prior steps complete
- Inferences — anchored to specific textual evidence. Beyond-text claims: flagged as speculative.
- Structural cues — paragraph breaks, transitions, headings each carry meaning. Document the organizational pattern before drawing conclusions.
- Source scope — single-text conclusions require a limitations statement. Cross-reference when multiple sources available.

**Rules**

- Step order fixed as 1→2→3→4→5→6. Follow sequentially — reordering excluded
- Inference anchoring — every inference requires a cited textual extract.
- Vocabulary check — words with uncertain meaning identified before analysis.
- Conclusion format — claim + evidence + reasoning chain, all three required.
