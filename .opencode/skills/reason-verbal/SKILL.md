## Procedure

- Survey the text — identify text type (narrative, argumentative, expository), structural markers, stated purpose, and scope. Note author and context.

- Extract literal content — identify main ideas, supporting claims, factual assertions, and key terms. Distinguish major from minor points. Create a proposition inventory.

- Map structure — trace relationships between parts: cause-effect, contrast, sequence, generalization-example. Identify transitions, topic shifts, and organizational pattern.

- Draw inferences — connect stated information to derive implicit conclusions. Identify assumptions the author relies on. Use → for inference chains.

- Evaluate critically — assess argument strength, evidence quality, author perspective, and potential biases. Distinguish fact from opinion. Identify rhetorical devices and their effect.

- Synthesize — produce a concise summary with key claims, evidence, and reasoning. State conclusions anchored to specific textual evidence.

## Gotchas

- Analysis progression follows order: literal extraction → structural mapping → inference → critical evaluation. Each step completes before the next. Evaluation after all prior steps complete
- Inferences — anchored to specific textual evidence. Beyond-text claims: flagged as speculative.
- Structural cues — paragraph breaks, transitions, headings each carry meaning. Document the organizational pattern before drawing conclusions.
- Source scope — single-text conclusions require a limitations statement. Cross-reference when multiple sources available.
- Skipping steps — follow the fixed order 1→2→3→4→5→6 sequentially; reordering loses the evidence chain
- Unanchored inference — cite the textual extract every inference rests on
- Uncertain vocabulary unexamined — identify words with uncertain meaning before analysis begins
- Conclusion missing a component — state claim, evidence, and reasoning chain; all three required
