**C Language** — a compiled, procedural, general-purpose programming language developed by Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs in 1972. Serves as the foundational language for systems programming, operating systems, and embedded software. Its design directly influenced C++, C#, Java, Go, Rust, and most modern C-family languages. Standardized as **ISO/IEC 9899**, with the current revision being C23 (ISO/IEC 9899:2024). Known for its minimal runtime, direct memory access through pointers, and terse syntax that maps closely to machine instructions. Remains one of the most widely used languages in systems software, firmware, and performance-critical applications.
