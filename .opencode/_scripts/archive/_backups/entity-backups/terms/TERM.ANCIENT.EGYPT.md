**Ancient Egypt** — a civilization of northeastern **Africa**, concentrated along the lower reaches of the **Nile River**, lasting from circa 3100 BCE (unification of Upper and Lower Egypt under the first pharaoh) to 30 BCE (Roman conquest). Organized into three principal kingdoms — **Old Kingdom** (2686–2181 BCE, the age of pyramid-building including the Great Pyramid of Giza under Pharaoh Khufu), **Middle Kingdom** (2055–1650 BCE, territorial expansion and literature), and **New Kingdom** (1550–1069 BCE, imperial zenith under pharaohs such as Hatshepsut, Akhenaten, Tutankhamun, and Ramesses II). The **pharaoh** served as absolute monarch and living incarnation of the god Horus. Hieroglyphic writing developed circa 3200 BCE; the **Rosetta Stone** (196 BCE) enabled its decipherment in 1822. Polytheistic religion centered on gods including Ra, Osiris, Isis, and Anubis, with elaborate mortuary practices including mummification and tomb construction. Advanced achievements in mathematics, medicine, astronomy, engineering, and irrigation. Fell successively to Persian, Macedonian (Ptolemaic), and Roman rule.
