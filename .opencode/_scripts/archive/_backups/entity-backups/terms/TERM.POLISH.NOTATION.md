**Polish notation** (**prefix notation**) — a mathematical notation in which operators precede their operands, eliminating the need for parentheses or precedence rules when each operator has a fixed arity. Invented by **Jan Łukasiewicz** in 1924 for propositional logic, it was later adopted in computer science for stack-based evaluation. The expression `+ 1 2` adds 1 and 2; nested expressions like `+ × 2 3 15` multiply 2 and 3 then add 15, read left-to-right with a stack. **Reverse Polish notation** (RPN, postfix) places operators after operands and was implemented in **Hewlett-Packard calculators** and the **Forth** language. Polish notation influenced **Lisp S-expressions**, where the prefix convention persists (e.g., `(+ 1 2)`), and appears in **Tcl**'s `mathop` library and **LDAP filter syntax**.
