**Constructive drawing** — a figure-drawing methodology that builds complex organic subjects from simplified three-dimensional forms (spheres, boxes, cylinders). Originating in the **Renaissance** structural approach and systematized in 20th-century atelier instruction, the method treats the human figure as a series of interlocking volumetric masses connected by gesture. Practitioners first establish proportion, landmarks (sternum, iliac crest, clavicles), and centerline; then layer primitive forms—the ribcage as an egg or box, the pelvis as a wedge, the neck and limbs as cylinders—to create a **mannequinized** armature. Construction provides the structural foundation for subsequent rendering, shading, and anatomical refinement. Key proponents include **Glenn Vilppu**, **Steve Huston**, and **Michael Hampton**, whose curricula underpin online platforms such as New Masters Academy and Proko.
