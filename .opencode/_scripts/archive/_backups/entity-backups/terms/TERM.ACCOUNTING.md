**Accounting** — the systematic process of identifying, measuring, recording, classifying, summarizing, and communicating financial information about economic entities to stakeholders including investors, creditors, management, and regulators. Originates in ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt (circa 4000 BCE) with clay-tablet transaction records. Modern accounting traces its foundation to **Luca Pacioli**'s 1494 publication _Summa de Arithmetica_, which codified **double-entry bookkeeping** — the system of debits and credits that remains the universal standard. Branches include **financial accounting** (external reporting under GAAP/IFRS), **management accounting** (internal decision-support), **tax accounting**, and **cost accounting**. Practitioners are known as **accountants**; the profession formalized in 19th-century Scotland with the first chartered accountants. Governed by conceptual frameworks issued by standard-setting bodies such as the **Financial Accounting Standards Board** (FASB) and the **International Accounting Standards Board** (IASB).
