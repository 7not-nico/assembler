**Parkour** — a discipline of traversing obstacles in man-made or natural environments through running, vaulting, jumping, climbing, rolling, and other movements — aiming to travel from one point to another in the quickest and most efficient way possible without equipment. Developed in France from the late 1980s, originating in the physical training methods of **Georges Hébert** ("la méthode naturelle") and later French military obstacle courses ("parcours du combattant"). Credited to **David Belle**, who trained with his father Raymond Belle (an elite firefighter) and co-founded the **Yamakasi** group with **Sébastien Foucan** in the Paris suburb of Lisses. Foucan later diverged to develop **freerunning**, which emphasizes artistry and acrobatics over efficiency. Practitioners are known as **traceurs**. Popularized globally through Internet videos, television commercials, and films including _Casino Royale_ (2006). Governing bodies include the **World Freerunning Parkour Federation** (2008) and **Mouvement International du Parkour** (2014). Recognized as a sport by the United Kingdom in 2016.
