**Music theory** — the study of the practices, structures, and principles underlying musical composition and performance. Rooted in the **Western common-practice period** (roughly 1600–1900) but extending across all musical traditions, its core topics include **pitch and notation** (staff, clefs, note values), **scales and keys** (major, minor, modal, chromatic), **intervals**, **rhythm and meter** (time signatures, beat division, syncopation), **harmony and voice leading** (chord construction, cadences, harmonic progression), **melody and counterpoint**, and **form** (binary, ternary, sonata, rondo, fugue). Foundational treatises include **Jean-Philippe Rameau**'s *Traité de l'harmonie* (1722) and **Heinrich Schenker**'s *Der freie Satz* (1935). Modern pedagogy synthesizes part-writing rules from the 18th-century species counterpoint tradition with jazz and popular-music harmony. Music theory is distinct from **ear training** and **sight singing**, though the three are typically taught together.
