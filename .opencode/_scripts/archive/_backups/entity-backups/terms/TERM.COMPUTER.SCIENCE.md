**Computer science** — the study of computation, information processing, and algorithmic systems, spanning both theoretical foundations and practical application. Its theoretical core comprises **algorithms and data structures**, **theory of computation** (automata, computability, computational complexity), and **information theory**. Applied branches include **programming languages**, **software engineering**, **artificial intelligence**, **computer graphics**, **networks**, and **computer security**. The field emerged mid-20th century from mathematics, electrical engineering, and logic, formalized by pioneers such as **Alan Turing**, **Alonzo Church**, **John von Neumann**, and **Grace Hopper**. Computer science is distinguished from programming by its emphasis on mathematical rigor — proving correctness, analyzing complexity, and establishing limits of what is computable. It underpins virtually all modern technology and is classified as a **STEM** discipline, with subfields organized under the **ACM Computing Classification System**.
