**Alpha equivalence (≡α)** — the congruence relation on lambda terms identifying terms that differ only by renaming of bound variables. Declares `λx.M ≡α λy.M[x:=y]` whenever `y ∉ FV(M)`. The relation `≡α` is the smallest congruence (reflexive, symmetric, transitive, closed under term structure) generated by this rule.

**Role in β-reduction** — β-reduction is confluent up to α-equivalence (Church–Rosser theorem). Two β-normal forms are equal if α-conversion can transform one into the other. Substitution is defined modulo α-equivalence: `(λy.M)[x:=N]` uses a fresh `y'` to avoid capture, ensuring the result is unique up to ≡α.

**α-equivalence classes** — all terms reachable via zero or more α-conversions form an equivalence class. Within a class, terms are considered identical for computational purposes.

**Examples:**
- `λx.x ≡α λy.y` (both identity)
- `λx.λy.x ≡α λa.λb.a` (K combinator)
- `λx.λy.y ≡α λa.λb.b` (KI combinator)
- `λx.x(λy.y) ≡α λa.a(λb.b)` (nested)
