**NIIF** — *Normas Internacionales de Información Financiera* (Spanish for International Financial Reporting Standards, IFRS), a single set of globally accepted accounting standards issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB). Two frameworks exist: **full NIIF** (~100+ standards and interpretations for public-interest entities) and **NIIF para PYMES** (simplified framework, ~35 sections, for small and medium-sized entities). All Spanish-speaking jurisdictions have adopted NIIF as their Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP). Since 2024, the IASB also issued **NIIF de Sostenibilidad** (NIIFS S1/S2) for sustainability-related financial disclosures, adopted in Costa Rica via Circular 33-23.

**NIIF en Costa Rica** — the *Colegio de Contadores Públicos de Costa Rica* (CCPCR) holds authority to adopt accounting standards under Art. 14, Law No. 1038 (19 Aug 1947). Timeline: partial NIC adoption in 1999 (Gaceta No. 197) → full adoption of NIC (renamed NIIF) on 27 Aug 2001 (Gaceta No. 167), effective for periods starting 1 Oct 2001 → Circular 06-2005 reaffirmed adoption and established automatic approval of future IASB amendments → Circular 06-2014 ratified full NIIF adoption → Circular 02-2018 (Gaceta No. 196, 24 Oct 2018) adopted NIIF para PYMES → Circular 33-23 (Dec 2023, Gaceta 10 Jan 2024) adopted NIIFS sustainability standards. CONASSIF (securities regulator) adopted NIIF in 2003 for regulated entities — financial institutions, pension operators, insurance companies, investment fund managers — which must use full NIIF. All other entities may choose full NIIF or NIIF para PYMES. Tax Code Art. 128 requires accounting under NIIF for tax purposes; the DGT (Dirección General de Tributación) issues interpretive criteria reconciling fiscal and accounting treatment. In 2026, DGT Resolution MH-DGT-RES-0015-2026 mandating full NIIF for large taxpayers was challenged by CCPCR via medida cautelar.
