**Quantum Mechanics** — The branch of physics describing nature at the smallest scales, where energy and matter exist in discrete states (quanta). From Latin *quantus* "how great, how much." Quantum mechanics formalizes the behavior of electrons, photons, and other particles through wave functions (Schrödinger equation), discrete energy levels, superposition, and probability amplitudes.

Quantum mechanics grounds the behavior of electrons in solids — the foundation of all semiconductor electronics. Band theory (energy bands in crystals), electron orbitals (allowed energy levels around atoms), and tunneling (electrons passing through barriers) are quantum phenomena. The classical computer's transistors depend on quantum mechanics: the band gap of silicon (~1.1 eV) determines its switching behavior. Without quantum mechanics, transistors are incomprehensible.
