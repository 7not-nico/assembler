**Electrical Engineering** — The engineering discipline concerned with the study and application of electricity, electronics, and electromagnetism. From Greek *ēlektron* "amber" (which produces static electricity when rubbed). Electrical engineering covers circuit theory (voltage, current, resistance — Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws), analog electronics (amplifiers, filters, signal processing), and power systems (generation, transmission, distribution).

Electrical engineering grounds the connection between physics and computation. Voltage (potential difference) and current (flow of charge) are the physical quantities that transistors manipulate. A transistor switch is an electrical device — applying a voltage to the gate controls current flow from source to drain. The classical computer is fundamentally an electrical machine: electrons moving through circuits at finite speed, dissipating heat, limited by the physics of electromagnetism.
