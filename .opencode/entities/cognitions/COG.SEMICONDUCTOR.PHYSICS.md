**Semiconductor Physics** — The branch of physics and materials science studying materials whose electrical conductivity lies between a conductor and an insulator, controllable through doping and electric fields. A semiconductor's conductivity is determined by its band gap — the energy difference between the valence band (electrons bound to atoms) and the conduction band (electrons free to move). Pure (intrinsic) semiconductors have low conductivity; doping introduces impurity atoms that donate (n-type) or accept (p-type) charge carriers, dramatically increasing conductivity.

Semiconductor physics grounds the transistor. The PN junction (interface between p-type and n-type silicon) is the fundamental building block — it conducts current in one direction only and can be switched on/off by an applied voltage. Silicon's band gap of ~1.12 eV makes it the dominant semiconductor material. Without semiconductor physics, there is no classical computer — only vacuum tubes and relays.
