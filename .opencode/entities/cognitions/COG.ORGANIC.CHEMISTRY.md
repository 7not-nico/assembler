**Organic Chemistry** — The branch of chemistry studying carbon-based compounds and their reactions. From Greek *organon* "tool, instrument" — originally the chemistry of compounds produced by living organisms, later defined by carbon as the central element. Carbon forms the chemical backbone of life: its ability to form four stable covalent bonds enables the chain, ring, and branching structures that make complex biomolecules possible.

Organic chemistry grounds biochemistry and molecular biology. The carbon atom (6 protons) bonds with hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur to form all the major classes of biomolecules: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. The functional groups — hydroxyl, carbonyl, carboxyl, amino, phosphate — are the reactive sites through which biochemical transformations occur.
