**Chronometrics** — The science of measuring time. From Greek *chronos* (χρόνος) "time" + *metrikos* (μετρικός) "of measurement." The empirical study of time's passage: the solar day (Earth's rotation), the lunar month (Moon's orbit), the solar year (Earth's revolution). Chronometrics provides the standards — second, minute, hour, day, week, month, year — that ground all temporal measurement.

The day is a chronometric form: Earth's rotation period relative to the sun (~24 hours) is a discovered astronomical fact, not a human invention. The week (~7 days) is a derived convention approximating the lunar phase cycle (29.5 days / 4 ≈ 7.4 days).
