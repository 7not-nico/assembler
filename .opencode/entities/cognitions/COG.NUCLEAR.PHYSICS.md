**Nuclear Physics** — The branch of physics studying atomic nuclei: protons, neutrons, and the strong force that binds them. From Latin *nucleus* "kernel, inner part." Nuclear physics investigates nuclear structure (shell model, liquid drop model), nuclear reactions (fission, fusion, radioactive decay), and the properties of subatomic particles.

Nuclear physics grounds the identity of elements. The number of protons in a nucleus — the atomic number — determines which element an atom is. Silicon has 14 protons; carbon has 6; gold has 79. Neutrons determine isotopes (same element, different mass). Without nuclear physics, there is no concept of "element" — only undifferentiated matter. The classical computer's material foundation (silicon wafers, dopant atoms, metal interconnects) is grounded in nuclear physics.
