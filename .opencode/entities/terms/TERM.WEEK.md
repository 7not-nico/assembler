**Week** — A cycle of seven consecutive days, derived from the lunar phase cycle. From Old English *wice*/*wucu* "a turning, succession," from Proto-Germanic *wikǭ* "turn," from PIE root *weyg-* "to bend, wind." The semantic arc: a winding → a turning of time → a cycle of days. The week's etymology *is* a cycle — "a winding succession" — mirroring its cyclic precede chain.

The 7-day week originates from the four quarters of the lunar month (~29.5 days / 4 ≈ 7.4 days). It was formalized by the Babylonians and adopted by Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. The Romans named days after celestial bodies and gods. TERM.WEEK is the encompassing term that brackets the Monday → Sunday cycle within CON.DAY.
