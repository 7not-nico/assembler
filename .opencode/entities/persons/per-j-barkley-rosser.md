**John Barkley Rosser** (1907–1989) — American mathematician and logician. Doctoral student of Alonzo Church at Princeton (Ph.D. 1934). Co-discovered the Church–Rosser theorem (1936), the fundamental confluence property of β-reduction in lambda calculus — if an expression reduces to two different forms, both reduce to a common form. Co-discovered the Kleene–Rosser paradox (1935), demonstrating inconsistency of Church's original system. Developed Rosser's trick, simplifying Gödel's incompleteness theorems by eliminating the ω-consistency condition. Contributed to combinatory logic, recursive function theory, and numerical analysis. Authored *A Mathematical Logic Without Variables* (1935) and the definitive historical survey *Highlights of the History of the Lambda-Calculus* (1984).
