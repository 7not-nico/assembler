**Alonzo Church** (1903–1995) — American mathematician, logician, and philosopher. Invented the lambda calculus (1932–1941) as a foundation for mathematics. Proved the unsolvability of the Entscheidungsproblem (Church's theorem, 1936), the first definitive negative solution to Hilbert's decision problem. Formulated the Church–Turing thesis identifying effective calculability with λ-definability and Turing computability. Co-discovered the Church–Rosser theorem (1936) on confluence of β-reduction. Developed Church encoding of data in the lambda calculus and the Frege–Church ontology in philosophical logic. Founding editor of the Journal of Symbolic Logic (1936–1979). Supervised 31 doctoral students including Turing, Kleene, Rosser, Scott, Rabin, Smullyan, and Henkin.
