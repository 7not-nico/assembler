**Haskell Brooks Curry** (1900–1982) — American mathematician and logician. Founder of combinatory logic, building on the work of Schönfinkel. Developed the theory of combinators (1930s), showing that bound variables can be eliminated entirely. The programming language Haskell is named after him. The technique of currying — transforming multi-argument functions into nested single-argument functions — bears his name (though discovered by Schönfinkel). Proved the Curry–Howard correspondence linking proofs to programs and types to propositions. Contributed to the Church–Rosser theorem with an alternative proof (1952). His foundational work in combinatory logic is a direct precursor and alternative to Church's lambda calculus.
