**Alan Mathison Turing** (1912–1954) — British mathematician, logician, and computer scientist. Invented the Turing machine (1936), the foundational model of computation, proving the unsolvability of the halting problem and the Entscheidungsproblem. Independently formulated the Church–Turing thesis and proved equivalence of Turing computability with λ-definability. Student of Alonzo Church at Princeton (Ph.D. 1938). During WWII, led Hut 8 at Bletchley Park, breaking the German Enigma cipher. Conceived the universal Turing machine, establishing the theoretical basis for stored-program computers. Proposed the Turing test for machine intelligence (1950). Founded the mathematical theory of biological morphogenesis (1952).
