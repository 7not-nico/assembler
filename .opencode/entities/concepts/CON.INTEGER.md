**Integer** — The discovered form of signed quantity. From Latin *integer* "whole, complete, intact" (from *in-* "not" + *tangere* "to touch" — untouched, undiminished). Integers extend the naturals by adding negative numbers: {... -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3 ...}. Every integer has an additive inverse — for every n there exists -n such that n + (-n) = 0.

Integers are discovered as soon as counting meets comparison: if one pile has five sheep and another has seven, the difference can be expressed. Negative numbers were formalized by Brahmagupta (628 CE) as "debts" (losses) opposed to "fortunes" (gains). The integer is the minimal set closed under subtraction — subtracting a larger from a smaller demands the negative.
