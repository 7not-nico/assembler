**Transistor** — The discovered form of the three-terminal semiconductor switch. From *transfer + resistor* — a device that transfers a signal through a variable resistance controlled by a third terminal. In a field-effect transistor (FET), voltage applied to the gate terminal controls current flow between source and drain terminals by modulating the conductivity of the semiconductor channel.

Transistors are discovered, not invented. The transistor effect — controlling current through a semiconductor using an electric field — was predicted by Julius Lilienfeld (1926) and demonstrated by Bardeen, Brattain, and Shockley at Bell Labs in 1947 (point-contact transistor, Nobel Prize 1956). The transistor is a discovered form because the physics of semiconductors inherently enables this behavior — a PN junction naturally acts as a voltage-controlled switch when configured correctly. Every classical computer is built from millions or billions of transistors organized into logic gates.
