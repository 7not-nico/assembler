**Machine Learning** — the study and construction of computer programs that improve their performance on tasks through experience. A mathematical discipline concerned with algorithms that extract patterns, models, and decision rules from data without being explicitly programmed for every case.

Tom Mitchell's formal definition: a computer program learns from experience E with respect to task T and performance measure P if its performance at T, as measured by P, improves with E (Mitchell 1997). This reduces to an optimization problem: minimize expected risk over a data distribution by adjusting parameters of a learned function.

Machine learning is not a physical process — it is an abstract mathematical procedure grounded in optimization theory, statistics, and information theory. It exists as a body of mathematics before any computer executes it. The distinction between machine learning (the concept) and machine code (the physical binary that implements it on hardware) is the clearing between concept and definition: one lives in the space of mathematical ideas, the other in the space of transistor states.
