**Sollers** — Latin *sollers* (gen. *sollertis*), from *sollus* "whole, entire" + *ars* "art, skill". Literally "full of art, full of skill." The direct structural antonym of *iners* (inert): *iners* = without art (*in-* + *ars*), *sollers* = full of art (*sollus* + *ars*).

Used by Cicero, Virgil, Ovid, Horace, and Livy to describe a person or thing that is skilled, clever, dexterous, ingenious — possessing the art to act effectively. Not merely "active" but *able through skill*.

The concept of *sollers* is a discovered distinction: some entities have the inherent power to act through their own art or skill; others lack it and are therefore inert. It is not invented — it is a property observable in nature and artifice alike. A river is *sollers* — it has the art to carve canyons. A watch is *sollers* — it has the art to measure time. A rock is *inert* — it has no art, no power to act.
