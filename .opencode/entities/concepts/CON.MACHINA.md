**Machina** — Latin *machina* (device, engine, contrivance), borrowed from Greek *mēkhanē* (device, tool, also "stage machinery" for *deus ex machina*). From PIE root `*magh-` "to be able, have power." Literally "that which has the power to do something."

Used by Latin authors to denote any contrivance that channels power into directed action. Where *ars* is skill in a maker, *machina* is the *thing made* that itself channels capacity. The root is not about mechanism but about *potency* — a machine is something that *can*.

The concept of *machina* is discovered, not invented. A lever is a *machina* — it has the power to multiply force. A clock is a *machina* — it has the power to measure time. The Antikythera device is a *machina* — its bronze gears have the power to compute celestial positions. English *machine* descends through French, entering c. 1540 meaning "any structure."
