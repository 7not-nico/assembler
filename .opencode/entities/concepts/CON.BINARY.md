**Binary** — The discovered form of two-valued representation. From Latin *bini* "two by two" + *-arius* "pertaining to." In mathematics, binary (base-2) represents every integer as a sum of powers of two using only the digits 0 and 1. Every integer has a unique binary representation — this is a theorem about the structure of numbers, not an invention.

Binary is discovered, not invented. Any system with two reliably distinguishable states can encode binary: voltage high/low (transistors), hole/no hole (punch cards), magnetic N/S (disk), light on/off (optical). The mathematical fact that two states suffice to represent any finite quantity is the foundation of digital computing. Leibniz (1703) described binary arithmetic in *Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire*, recognizing that the *I Ching*'s hexagrams (China, ~1000 BCE) independently discovered binary representation.

Binary grounds the encoding of all data in classical computers: instructions (machine code), addresses, numbers, characters, and media. The bit (binary digit) is the fundamental unit of information in classical computing.
