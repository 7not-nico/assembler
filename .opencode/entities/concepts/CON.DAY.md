**Day** — The discovered form of Earth's solar rotation period. From Old English *dæg* "the period of sunlight," from Proto-Germanic *dagaz*, from PIE root *dʰegʷʰ-* "to burn" — the period of the sun's burning across the sky. A day is the time Earth takes to complete one rotation relative to the sun: approximately 24 hours (86,400 seconds precisely). The day is a discovered astronomical form, not an invention — Earth rotates, creating day and night, whether or not any being measures it.

The day grounds all shorter time units (hour, minute, second) and combines into longer units. The 7-day week is a human convention derived from the lunar cycle, bracketed as TERM.WEEK within the day concept.
