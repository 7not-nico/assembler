**Real Number** — The discovered form of the continuum. From Latin *realis* "actual, genuine" — the real numbers are the union of rational and irrational numbers, forming the complete ordered field. Every real number corresponds to a point on the number line, filling every gap between rationals. Formalized by Dedekind (cuts, 1872) and Cantor (Cauchy sequences, 1883).

The reals are discovered wherever continuity exists: the position of a particle in space, the flow of time, the magnitude of a physical quantity. The continuum is not invented by mathematicians but revealed by the physical world's structure — length, area, volume are all continuous quantities that demand the real numbers for their full expression. In computing, reals are approximated by floating-point representation (IEEE 754), trading completeness for finiteness.
