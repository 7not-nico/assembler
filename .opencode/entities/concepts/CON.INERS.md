**Iners** — Latin *iners* (gen. *inertis*), from *in-* "without" + *ars* "art, skill". Literally "without art, without skill." The direct structural antonym of *sollers*: *sollers* = full of art (*sollus* + *ars*), *iners* = without art (*in-* + *ars*).

Used by Roman authors to describe something inactive, sluggish, inert — lacking the power to act through its own skill. Where *sollers* denotes the capacity to act artfully, *iners* denotes its absence. The English word *inert* derives from it.

The concept of *iners* is a discovered distinction: some entities lack the inherent power to act through their own art. They are passive — they are acted upon, they do not act. A rock is *iners* — it has no art, no power to act. Machine code is *iners* — binary patterns that must be read by a CPU before any computation occurs. The CPU is *sollers* (it has the art to fetch-decode-execute); the code it reads is *iners*.
