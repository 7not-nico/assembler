**Crystal** — The discovered form of ordered atomic arrangement. From Greek *krystallos* "ice, rock crystal" — a solid whose constituent atoms, molecules, or ions are arranged in a regularly repeating three-dimensional lattice. The crystal lattice is defined by its unit cell (the smallest repeating unit) and the bonding between atoms (covalent, ionic, metallic, or molecular).

Crystals are discovered, not invented. The world presents minerals as faceted solids with regular internal structure. Silicon crystallizes in the diamond cubic structure — each silicon atom covalently bonds to four neighbors in a tetrahedral arrangement. This perfect periodicity is essential for semiconductor behavior: the band gap, carrier mobility, and dopant substitution all depend on the crystal lattice being regular and essentially defect-free.
