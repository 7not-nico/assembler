**Semiconductor** — The discovered form of controlled-conductivity material. From Latin *semi-* "half" + *conducere* "to lead together" — a material whose electrical conductivity lies between a conductor (metal) and an insulator, controllable through doping and external voltage. A pure semiconductor (intrinsic) has few free charge carriers; doping introduces impurity atoms that donate (n-type) or accept (p-type) carriers.

Semiconductors are discovered, not invented. The rectifying effect of certain crystals (galena, silicon) was observed by early radio engineers (Ferdinand Braun, 1874). The physics of band gaps, charge carriers, and PN junctions was worked out in the 1930s-1940s (Bardeen, Shockley, Brattain). The key insight: some materials have a band gap small enough that doping can dramatically change their conductivity — this is a discovered property of the material's electronic structure.
