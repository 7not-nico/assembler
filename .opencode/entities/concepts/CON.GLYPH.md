**Glyph** — a carved or formed mark, the conclusion of a making act. From Greek *glyphē* "carving" (*glýphein* "to carve, engrave"). A glyph is a physical trace — strokes cut into stone, pixels on a screen, ink on paper. It exists regardless of whether anyone knows its meaning. A glyph becomes a symbol only when a shared context assigns signification to it.

Glyphs are distinct from symbols: a glyph is the mark side of the mark-meaning pair. A symbol is what the mark means; a glyph is the mark itself. The same glyph can carry different symbols in different contexts. A glyph without known context is a mark awaiting meaning — it can be re-assigned new signification.
