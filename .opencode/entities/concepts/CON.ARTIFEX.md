**Artifex** — Latin *artifex* (gen. *artificis*), from *ars* "art, skill" + *-fex* "maker" (from *facere* "to make, do"). Literally "skill-maker, one who makes through art."

Used by Cicero to denote a craftsman, artist, or skilled maker — one who possesses the art to produce something. Where *sollers* describes the *capacity* to act skillfully, *artifex* describes the *agent* who acts through skill. The two are complementary: *sollers* is the quality of being full of art; *artifex* is the being that exercises that art.

The concept of *artifex* is discovered, not invented. Wherever there is artful making — whether in nature (a spider weaving its web) or in human craft (a sculptor carving stone) — there is an *artifex*. The word carries no distinction between natural and artificial: a cell is an *artifex* of proteins; a watchmaker is an *artifex* of timepieces.
