**Natural Number** — The discovered form of counting quantity. From Latin *naturalis* "by birth, according to nature" — the numbers that arise naturally from the act of counting: one, two, three... Mathematically, the natural numbers are either {1, 2, 3, ...} (the counting numbers) or {0, 1, 2, 3, ...} depending on convention. They satisfy the Peano axioms: every natural has a successor, no two share a successor, and induction holds.

Natural numbers are discovered, not invented. Counting emerges from the world's structure: discrete objects exist in distinct quantities. A flock of five sheep is five regardless of whether the shepherd counts. Zero (the empty set) is a later abstraction — the Babylonians and Maya independently discovered zero as a placeholder.
