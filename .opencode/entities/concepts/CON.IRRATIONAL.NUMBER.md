**Irrational Number** — The discovered form of incommensurable quantity. From Latin *in-* "not" + *rationalis* "reasonable, of ratio" — a number that cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers, equivalent to a non-terminating, non-repeating decimal. The first irrational discovered was √2 by the Pythagorean school (c. 500 BCE), which Hippasus allegedly proved by geometric reasoning.

Irrationals are discovered whenever measurement reveals incomparable magnitudes. The diagonal of a unit square is √2 — it exists as a length, but no integer ratio captures it. π is the ratio of circumference to diameter — it exists as a geometric fact, but no fraction expresses it. Irrationals are not errors or approximations — they are exact mathematical objects discovered through the structure of space, geometry, and limits. Together with rationals they comprise the real numbers.
