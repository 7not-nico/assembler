**Programma** — Greek *programma* (πρόγραμμα), from *pro-* "forth" + *graphein* "to scratch, carve, write" (from PIE `*gerbh-` "to scratch, carve"). Literally "something written forth, a public notice."

In classical Athens, a *programma* was a written proclamation posted publicly — an edict, a notice, a schedule. The word passed into Latin as *programma* (proclamation, edict), then into English (1630s) as "public notice." The sense shifted to "a definite plan or scheme" (1837), then to "broadcasting presentation" (1923), then to "series of coded instructions directing a computer" (1945).

The semantic arc: something scratched forth for public view → a planned sequence → instructions for a machine. The root *graphein* (to scratch, carve) preserves the physicality of the earliest inscriptions — programming is carving instructions into a medium the machine reads. A programmer is one who scratches forth.
