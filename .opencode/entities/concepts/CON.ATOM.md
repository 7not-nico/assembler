**Atom** — The discovered form of discrete elemental matter. From Greek *atomos* "uncuttable, indivisible" — the smallest unit of a chemical element that retains its properties. An atom consists of a nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electrons in probabilistic orbitals (quantum mechanical cloud). The number of protons (atomic number) uniquely identifies the element.

Atoms are discovered, not invented. The world presents matter as clumped into discrete units — grain by grain, drop by drop. Leucippus and Democritus (5th century BCE) reasoned that matter must be composed of indivisible particles. Dalton (1803) established atomic theory empirically. The atom is a discovered form because matter is actually discrete — elements come in countable units, 14 protons means silicon, not carbon.
