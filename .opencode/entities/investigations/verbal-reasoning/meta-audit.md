# Verbal Reasoning — Cross-Region Meta-Audit

**Investigation:** `verbal-reasoning`
**Date:** 2026-07-12
**Sub-concepts:** Reading comprehension, Critical reasoning, Text completion & sentence equivalence, Vocabulary & semantic relationships, Logical verbal reasoning, Rhetorical structure & discourse

---

## 1. Fundamentals

### Definitions

| Source | Definition | Source Type |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| **ETS — GRE Verbal Reasoning** | Ability to analyze and evaluate written material, synthesize information, analyze relationships among sentence parts, recognize relationships among words and concepts | Standardized assessment (US) |
| **PISA / OECD** | Capacity to understand, use and reflect on written texts to achieve personal goals, develop knowledge and potential, and participate in society | International framework |
| **PIRLS / IEA** | Ability to use and understand written language formats required by society and/or valued by the individual; construct meaning from texts | International framework |
| **Simple View of Reading** | Reading comprehension = decoding × language comprehension — both necessary, neither sufficient alone | Cognitive theory |
| **Kintsch Construction-Integration Model** | Comprehension at three levels: surface structure, textbase, situation model — reader constructs mental representation | Cognitive theory |
| **Reading Systems Framework (Perfetti & Stafura)** | Two subprocesses: word identification (orthographic/phonological/semantic) and word-to-text integration | Cognitive framework |

### Core Skills (synthesized across regions)

1. **Literal comprehension** — retrieving explicitly stated information, identifying main ideas, following sequence
2. **Inferential comprehension** — drawing conclusions from incomplete data, connecting ideas, filling gaps
3. **Critical/evaluative comprehension** — identifying author assumptions, perspective, strengths/weaknesses of arguments
4. **Structural analysis** — understanding text organization, transitions, relationships between parts
5. **Vocabulary & semantic relationships** — word meanings, roots, affixes, connotations, relationships among concepts
6. **Synthesis & summarization** — integrating information, distinguishing major from minor points, condensing meaning

## 2. By Region

### US/UK — PASS (5/5 academic)
- GRE Verbal Reasoning is the dominant framework: Reading Comprehension, Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence
- Reading comprehension research (Reading Rockets) emphasizes active meaning construction, cognitive and metacognitive strategies
- ETS provides the most structured assessment framework for verbal reasoning skills
- Key institutions: ETS (.org), Reading Rockets / WETA (.org)

### Western Europe — PASS (5/5 academic)
- Reading comprehension theory is well-established: Kintsch CI model, SVR, RSF, PIRLS framework
- PIRLS defines four comprehension processes: retrieve, infer, interpret/evaluate, critique
- Simple View of Reading (Gough & Tunmer) foundational: decoding × language comprehension
- Key institutions: Springer, IEA / Boston College, EBSCO

### Scandinavia — PASS (5/5 academic)
- PISA reading literacy: Finland 490 (OECD avg 476); gender gap 45 points favoring girls
- Critical literacy research at University of Jyväskylä (Sirén & Sulkunen 2023)
- PISA reading measured across 81 countries as international benchmark
- Key institutions: OECD, University of Jyväskylä, NCES

### India — WARN (commercial-heavy)
- India uses "Verbal Ability and Reading Comprehension" (VARC), not "Verbal Reasoning"
- CAT exam VARC: Reading Comprehension + Verbal Ability (parajumbles, odd sentences, summary)
- Ecosystem is dominated by commercial exam-prep publishers (Pearson, McGraw Hill, coaching platforms)
- Minimal academic (.ac.in) research on verbal reasoning as a construct
- Verbal ability topics: parajumbles, sentence elimination, paragraph summary, paragraph completion

### East Asia — WARN (limited scope)
- Research focuses on L2 (English) reading comprehension, not native-language verbal reasoning
- Nagoya University: L1 literacy as strong predictor of L2 reading comprehension
- J-STAGE: AI tools for generating reading comprehension questions in Japanese EFL context
- Korea University: reading-writing connection for EFL learners
- Limited research on "verbal reasoning" as a labeled construct

## 3. Gaps

### Region-Level Gaps
| Region | Gap Detail |
|--------|------------|
| India | No academic research on verbal reasoning as construct — entire ecosystem is commercial exam prep |
| East Asia | Verbal reasoning not studied as independent construct; subsumed under EFL reading comprehension |
| Scandinavia | Verbal reasoning framed as PISA reading literacy — critical literacy research limited to specific sub-skills |
| Western Europe | Strong reading comprehension theory but no "verbal reasoning" label — framed as reading literacy |
| US/UK | ETS framework is comprehensive but assessment-driven; less research on teaching verbal reasoning skills |

### Cross-Cutting Gaps
1. **No unified definition** — "Verbal reasoning" means different things across regions: GRE-style test skills (US), reading comprehension (Europe), verbal ability (India), EFL reading (East Asia)
2. **Assessment vs instruction** — Most resources focus on measuring verbal reasoning (ETS, PISA, PIRLS, CAT) rather than teaching the underlying cognitive skills
3. **Critical literacy gap** — PISA and PIRLS include critical evaluation but research shows it's underrepresented (Sirén & Sulkunen 2023: PISA critical literacy items mostly ignored social justice aspects)
4. **Vocabulary connection** — Vocabulary knowledge is known to be central to reading comprehension (lexical quality hypothesis, Perfetti 2007) but underemphasized in verbal reasoning instruction
5. **L1-L2 transfer** — Nagoya University research shows L1 literacy strongly predicts L2 reading comprehension, but this is rarely incorporated into verbal reasoning curricula
