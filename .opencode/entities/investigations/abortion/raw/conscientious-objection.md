# Conscientious Objection and Physician Oath Traditions — Deep Dive

## Overview

The Hippocratic Oath's original clause *"I will not give a woman a pessary to cause an abortion"* is the historical root of the physician-life-preservation stance. However, this clause reflected a minority Pythagorean view among ancient Greek physicians — most practiced abortion freely. Modern medical oaths have removed the clause, yet the ethical tension persists.

~80 countries explicitly allow conscientious objection (CO) to abortion. The mechanism for navigating this tension operates at four levels: statutory law, professional ethics codes, institutional policy, and individual conscience.

## Original Hippocratic Oath Text (c. 5th-3rd c. BCE)

*"I will not give a woman a pessary to cause an abortion."*

Key context from Queen's University MA thesis (2022, "The Hippocratic Stance on Abortion"):
- The oath's abortion prohibition was anomalous in ancient Greek medicine
- Most Greek and Roman physicians performed or prescribed abortion
- The oath reflected Pythagorean religious beliefs, not medical consensus
- Post-Christianity, the clause was reinforced and universalized
- Nearly all modern versions remove the clause

## Modern Oath Variants

| Oath | Year | Abortion stance | Notes |
|------|------|----------------|-------|
| Original Hippocratic | c. 400 BCE | Prohibits | "I will not give a woman a pessary to cause an abortion" |
| Declaration of Geneva | 1948 (rev. 2017) | Silent | "Utmost respect for human life" — deliberately ambiguous |
| AMA Code of Medical Ethics | 1847 (rev. ongoing) | Neutral | Abortion recognized as legal medical procedure; CO permitted with continuity-of-care duty |
| ACOG Committee Opinion | 2016 (rev. ongoing) | Supportive | "Safe, legal abortion is a necessary component of women's health" |
| UK Abortion Act 1967 s.4 | 1967 | Statutory CO | "No person shall be under any duty to participate"; emergency exception |
| WHO Abortion Care Guideline | 2022 | CO must not impede | States must regulate CO to ensure access; referral duty; emergency exception |
| Islamic medical ethics | Various | Conditional | Abortion permitted before 120 days on certain grounds per most schools |
| Catholic medical ethics | 1995 (Evangelium Vitae) | Prohibits | Direct abortion intrinsically evil; no exceptions |

## CO Regulation Typology

Based on Chavkin et al. (2013) international comparative study and de Londras et al. (2023) systematic review:

| Regulatory model | Examples | Key features |
|-----------------|----------|--------------|
| Uncodified (no CO right) | Sweden, Finland, Turkey, Iceland | CO not mentioned in law; interpreted as no right to refuse |
| Statutory CO with limits | UK, Portugal, Norway, France | CO permitted but with referral duty, emergency exception, limited scope |
| Broad CO (weak regulation) | Italy, Poland, Croatia, Hungary | CO permitted without strong access safeguards; high CO rates create de facto access barriers |
| Institution-level CO | Many US Catholic hospitals | Entire institutions refuse; creates access deserts |
| Post-conflict regulation | Colombia, Argentina (post-legalization) | New legal frameworks mandate non-objecting providers, referral systems |

## Key Academic Positions

**Pro-CO (limited right):**
- WHO and UN: CO permitted but must not impede access. Referral duty and emergency exception required.
- UK General Medical Council: "Patients must not be denied access to appropriate medical treatment or advice"

**Anti-CO (no right to refuse):**
- Savulescu & Schuklenk (2017, *Bioethics*): "Doctors have no right to refuse medical assistance in dying, abortion or contraception"
  - Argument: doctors are not conscripts but chose the profession knowing these procedures exist
  - Professional obligations override personal conscience in core service provision

**Conscientious Commitment (provision as moral duty):**
- Giubilini, Schuklenk, Minerva, Savulescu (2024, *J Med Ethics*): doctors may be *professionally* justified providing abortions even where illegal
  - Argument: if the patient's best medical interest tracks the physician's conscience, professional bodies should protect the provider
  - Claims of conscientious commitment may be *stronger* than conscientious refusal because they respond to an autonomous patient request with clear interests

**Context Matters:**
- Harris et al. (2016, *Global Public Health*): Social, economic, and political pressures profoundly influence CO claims
  - Low pay, high workload, stigma, and unclear policies can drive CO claims even without sincere moral objection
  - CO functions as a "safety valve" for clinicians under systemic pressure

## CO Impact Summary (de Londras et al. 2023)

| Finding | Evidence |
|---------|----------|
| CO causes delay in care | Documented across multiple high-CO jurisdictions (Italy, Poland) |
| CO increases stigma | Both for patients seeking care and providers offering it |
| CO creates access deserts | Regional shortages where CO rates exceed ~50% |
| CO harms non-objecting providers | Increased workload, moral distress, stigmatization |
| CO extends beyond opt-out | Includes dissuasion, misinformation, misdirection, sometimes abuse |
| Referral systems often fail | "Conscientious referral" not consistently implemented |
| Emergency exceptions inconsistently applied | Anesthesiologists refusing even for missed abortion/miscarriage management |

## Notable Case: Anesthesiologist Refusal (US)

- 69% of OB-Gyn residency program directors report opposition to providing abortion in practice settings
- 30% of opposition attributed specifically to anesthesiologists
- Documented cases: anesthesiologist refused to provide anesthesia for missed abortion management (fetal demise) — overbroad application of CO
- Overbroad CO: refusal based on procedure type rather than clinical details (e.g., refusing all D&Cs regardless of indication)

## Open Questions / Gaps

1. No global registry of oath variants — which medical schools include/exclude abortion language?
2. No standardized methodology for measuring CO rates across countries
3. Correlation between oath language and actual CO behavior untested
4. Impact of post-Dobbs environment on US CO rates unmeasured
5. CO in non-Western medical traditions (Ayurveda, Unani, Traditional Chinese Medicine) unindexed
6. Relationship between CO rates and unsafe-abortion outcomes not quantified cross-regionally
