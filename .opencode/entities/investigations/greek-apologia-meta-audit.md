## 1. Fundamentals

### Etymology & Definition
- **apologia** (ἀπολογία) ← *apologos* (ἀπόλογος) "a speech in defense" ← *apo* "away" + *logos* "speech"
- Verb: *apologeisthai* (ἀπολογεῖσθαι) "to speak in one's defense"
- NOT an apology (expression of regret) — rather a reasoned defense of position/action
- Source: Wikipedia EN; ThoughtCo; Ware & Linkugel 1973

### Classical Greek Context
- Originated in Athenian judicial procedure: *kategoria* (accusation) → *apologia* (defense)
- Practiced by Attic orators: Antiphon, Lysias, Isocrates, Demosthenes, Andocides, Isaeus, Aeschines
- Plato, Isocrates, and Aristotle described apologia as an oratory genre
- Aristotle classified it under forensic (dicanic) rhetoric in *Rhetoric*
- Primary exemplar: Plato's *Apology of Socrates* (399 BCE)

### Core Principles (Socratic)
1. **Truth-telling** — apologia as vehicle for truth, not persuasion
2. **Anti-rhetoric** — Socrates disavows rhetorical skill while deploying it masterfully
3. **Elenchos (refutation)** — dialectical questioning as defensive method
4. **Erotesis (cross-examination)** — interrogating accuser (Meletos) rather than presenting evidence
5. **Human wisdom** — knowledge of ignorance (*anthropine sophia*)
6. **Divine mission** — philosophical activity as service to Apollo
7. **Care of the soul** (*epimeleia heautou*) — virtue over wealth/reputation

Sources: Plato, *Apology*; Dorion 1990; Pozzoni (ousia.it); Natterer; Wikilivres
