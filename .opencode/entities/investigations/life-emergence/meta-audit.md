# Life Emergence: From Simple Premises to Complex Systems

## Fundamentals
Life is conceptualized not as a biological accident, but as a **natural phase transition** in the evolution of the universe. It emerges when chemical networks reach a critical threshold of diversity and complexity, transitioning from non-living matter to self-sustaining, evolvable systems.

### Key Theoretical Frameworks
- **First-Order Phase Transition**: Life's emergence is modeled as a dynamic shift where replicators couple with their environment, redistributing matter and exhibiting explosive growth in diversity (Mathis et al., 2017).
- **Kantian Wholes & Closure**: Living organisms achieve a state of mutual dependency between parts and the whole through Catalytic, Constraint, and Spatial Closure (Kauffman & Roli, 2024).
- **The Adjacent Possible**: Molecular diversity increases over time, exponentially expanding the set of possible new molecules, which eventually triggers a phase transition to molecular reproduction.
- **Collectively Autocatalytic Sets (CAS)**: The unification of CAS and the Theory of the Adjacent Possible suggests that life is an expected outcome of chemical evolution.
