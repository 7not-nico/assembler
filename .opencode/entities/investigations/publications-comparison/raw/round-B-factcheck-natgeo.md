# Round B — National Geographic Fact-Checking

**Status:** PASS (historic) / WARN (current)

## Historic editorial process (pre-2023)

- Industry gold standard for decades
- 2016 Innovation Media article: 2-year lead time for major features
- Story teams: writers, photographers, designers, editors as equals
- Multiple rounds of editorial review + expert critique + research review
- Graphics: up to 6 months production, technical checks for color/crops/line widths
- Expert vetting of all scientific content
- Fact-checking department with dedicated researchers

## Current state (post-2023 layoffs)

- **All 19 staff writers laid off** (June 2023)
- Editorial work outsourced to freelance writers
- Few editors remain on staff
- Audio department eliminated
- Newsstand sales ended in US
- Formal fact-checking infrastructure likely degraded
- NatGeo spokesperson claimed "quality of storytelling" unchanged

## Corrections

- Corrections page exists: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/pages/article/corrections
- Minimal — only provides email address (editor@natgeo.com)
- No public corrections log or list of past corrections

## Independent ratings

- No current MBFC rating available
- Historically high factual reporting reputation
- Multiple Pulitzers in earlier decades — none in recent years

## Key concern

The transition from staff-written, fact-checked journalism to freelance-driven model raises significant questions about current editorial rigor. No public documentation of current fact-checking standards.
