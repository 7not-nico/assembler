# Meta-Audit: What Does a Liberal Arts Education Give?
**Investigation**: liberal-arts-education
**Date**: 2026-07-12
**Status**: PASS (all regions)

## Fundamentals

Liberal arts education — cross-culturally known as:
- US/UK: liberal education / liberal arts
- Germany: Studium Generale / Geisteswissenschaftliche Bildung
- France: Humanités / Formation Générale
- Japan: リベラルアーツ (riberaru ātsu)

**Core outcomes shared across all 4 regions:**

| Outcome | US/UK | Germany | France | Japan |

## Meta-Analyses

**AAC&U LEAP Report** (US): 15-year research program documenting employer demand for liberal education outcomes. Consistently finds:
- 9/10 employers value critical thinking, communication, teamwork
- Cross-cutting skills > undergraduate major
- Civic engagement lowest-valued by older employers, rising among younger

**Wabash National Study** (US, multi-institution longitudinal):
- Liberal arts colleges → indirect cognitive gains via instructional clarity + deep learning
- Interactional diversity → significant critical thinking growth
- Deep learning (integrative, reflective, higher-order) → need for cognition

**SG Netzwerk Nord** (Germany): Collective charter defining Studium Generale outcomes — interdisciplinary discourse, reflection, social responsibility, lifelong learning

**French Competency Framework** (national): Shift from content-based to competency-based humanities education; transversal skills explicitly codified

## By Region

### US/UK — PASS
- Strongest empirical literature (AAC&U employer surveys, Wabash longitudinal, UChicago noncognitive)
- Employer evidence: liberal arts graduates strongly positioned for workforce
- Civic engagement well-documented but less prioritized by older employers
- **Key sources**: AAC&U, Wabash College, UChicago Consortium, UWisc

### Germany — PASS
- Humboldtian Bildung tradition: unity of formation and training
- Studium Generale as post-WWII democratic rebuilding tool
- Strong on key qualifications (Schlüsselqualifikationen) and social participation
- **Key sources**: HCU Hamburg, Uni Mainz, Uni Siegen, Uni Würzburg

### France — PASS
- Most structured competency framework across regions
- Pluridisciplinarity as explicit design principle (4 disciplines across all years)
- Elite limited-intake programs (Strasbourg 30/yr)
- Rhetoric and argumentation uniquely emphasized
- **Key sources**: Strasbourg, Nanterre, Lille, Lorraine

### Japan — PASS
- Liberal arts + career education explicitly linked
- Critical thinking taught via debate, discussion, argument analysis (Kyoto, Yamaguchi)
- Multi-perspective (多角的) and bird's-eye (俯瞰) thinking uniquely named
- Employer feedback: value autonomy, flexibility, communication
- **Key sources**: Kyoto University, Tokyo Women's Christian, Teikyo, Ritsumeikan

## Cross-Cutting Gaps

| Gap | Regions Affected |
|-----|-----------------|
| Causal evidence isolating liberal arts from general higher education | All — US has strongest but still limited |
| Longitudinal tracking of liberal arts graduates vs. specialized | All |
| Civic engagement outcome measurement | All — employers deprioritize; academics value |
| Equity of access to liberal arts education | France (elite-limited), Japan (supplement model) |
| Standardized cross-national outcome comparisons | All — no framework exists |
