# Pedagogy: Interactional Diversity
## Region: US/UK
## Source investigation: liberal-arts-education

## Rating: PASS

## Mechanism

Interactional diversity — the amount and extent to which diverse groups of students, faculty, and administrators interact in and out of class (Gurin, 1999). Not mere compositional diversity (numerical representation), but *informal engagement* across racial, ethnic, political, and religious difference.

## Evidence

| Source | Finding | Design |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Wabash National Study (Pascarella & Blaich) | Interactional diversity was the **only** practice with significant positive impact on ALL 3 cognitive outcomes (critical thinking, need for cognition, positive attitude toward literacy) — net of 6 other good practices and confounds | Multi-institution longitudinal, 4-year, standardized CAAP CT test |
| Gurin (1999) | Diverse learning environment → complex social structure → active thinking processes; students with most diverse interactions are most engaged | National longitudinal |
| NSSE multi-institution (Pike & Kuh) | Student-body diversity indirectly related to gains via *informal interactional diversity*; compositional diversity → more cross-group interaction → learning gains | National sample, 428 institutions |
| UMD/NSSE (2005) | Positive perception of supportive racial campus climate → predictor of interactional diversity; seniors perceive less supportive climate | NSSE data, COPLAC + Virginia peers |

## Key Causal Pathway

```
Compositional diversity → Informal interactional diversity → Cognitive gains (critical thinking, openness, intellectual engagement)
```

Interactional diversity mediates the diversity → critical thinking link. Mere structural diversity insufficient without meaningful cross-group contact.

## Gaps
- Causal direction not fully resolved (self-selection: open-minded students seek diverse interactions?)
- Effect size moderate — one practice among several; other good practices (clear instruction, deep learning) also matter
- Less evidence on *quality* vs. *quantity* of interaction
