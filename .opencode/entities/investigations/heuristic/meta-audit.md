# Meta-Audit: HEURISTIC

**3 regions PASS**

## Etymological Chain

PIE `*wreh₁-` "to find" → Greek εὑρίσκω (heurískō) "to find, discover" → Neo-Latin *heuristicus* → English *heuristic* (1821)

Archimedes: εὕρηκα (heureka) "I have found it"

## Regional Summaries

### Greek — PASS
- εὑρίσκω from PIE *wreh₁- "to find"
- εὑρετικός (Plato): "inventive, of a discoverer"
- Semantic arc: physical search → mental perception → judgment
- Root metaphor: **discovery** — active, probing, not guaranteed

### Anglophone — PASS
- Term coined 1821 (irregular formation from Greek)
- Polya (1945): "How to Solve It" — heuristic as problem-solving method
- Simon/Newell (1950s): General Problem Solver, bounded rationality
- Kahneman/Tversky (1970s): heuristics & biases (cognitive shortcuts with errors)
- Gigerenzer (1990s): fast & frugal heuristics (ecologically rational)

### German — PASS
- Polya → Duncker (Gestalt) → Selz → Gigerenzer (MPI)
- **Heuristik** = "Kunst, mit begrenztem Wissen zu guten Lösungen zu kommen"
- Core contrast: Heuristic ≠ algorithm. Not guaranteed optimal. Sufficient for purpose.

## Comparison Table

| Region | Term | Root | Metaphor | Key figure | Guarantee? |
|--------|------|------|----------|------------|------------|
| Greek | εὑρίσκω | *wreh₁-* "find" | **Discovery** | Archimedes | No |

**Unifying thread**: heuristic is the only one of the four that is *fallible*. Principle, maxim, tenet are held as true; heuristic is used as a tool, tested by results, not by correspondence to truth.
