## Key Findings

### MAXIM in Kant (Maxime)
- Kant's definition (Grundlegung, AA 04: 400n): "Maxime ist das subjektive Prinzip des Wollens" — the subjective principle of volition
- Distinction: **Prinzip** = objective (valid for all); **Maxime** = subjective (valid only for the agent)
- Maxime = the agent's self-imposed rule of action — "When in S-type situations, perform A-type acts"
- Categorical imperative tests maxims: "Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law"
- Three elements of maxims (Potter): (1) moral character, (2) end/goal, (3) kind of action

### PRINZIP in Kant
- Kant uses Grundsatz and Prinzip interchangeably (translator's note, KpV)
- "Prinzipien a priori" — principles known independent of experience
- Distinction: theoretical principles (laws of nature) vs practical principles (laws of freedom)

### Semantic Architecture
- Prinzip — objective, universal, a priori → corresponds to Latin principium
- Maxime — subjective, agent-relative, self-imposed → corresponds to Latin maxima (but transformed)
- Grundsatz — foundational proposition, literally "ground-sentence"
