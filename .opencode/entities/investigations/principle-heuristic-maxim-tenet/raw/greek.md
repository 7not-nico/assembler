## Key Findings

### HEURISTIC (heuriskein)
- Greek root: εὑρίσκω (heurískō) "I find, discover, devise, invent, get, gain, procure"
- PIE root: *wreh₁- "to find" (Beekes); also reconstructed as *u̯er- or *were-(2)
- Derivations: εὕρημα "find, discovery", εὕρεσις "invention", εὑρετής "discoverer", εὑρετικός "inventive"
- Famous: Archimedes' εὕρηκα (heureka) "I have found (it)"
- English term "heuristic" (1821) — irregular formation from heuriskein + -istic
- Core metaphor: finding/discovering through search — not guaranteed, not optimal

### Semantic development of εὑρίσκω (La Roi 2020)
1. Homeric: physical finding (people, things in space) — 50 occurrences
2. Classical: mental perception finding — discovering a fact previously unknown
3. Classical: mental judgment finding — forming an opinion ("I find that...")
4. Metaphorical extension: physical search → mental search → judgment

### Distinction from principle/maxim/tenet
- Heuristic derives from *act of finding* — active, discovery-oriented
- Unlike principle (foundation) or tenet (held belief) or maxim (greatest proposition)
- The -iskō suffix is inchoative — denotes *entering into* a state of finding
