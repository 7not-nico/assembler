# Meta-Audit: Principle, Heuristic, Maxim, Tenet

**Date**: 2026-07-19
**Method**: Regional source survey (Latin/Italic, Greek, German, Anglophone)
**Status**: COMPLETE — 4/4 regions PASS

## Fundamentals

All four terms are meta-propositional — they name types of statements that guide thought or action. Their semantic differences arise from distinct root metaphors in their source languages.

| Term | Source Language | Root | Literal Meaning | Root Metaphor |
|------|----------------|------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Principle** | Latin *principium* | *primus* + *capere* | "first-taking" | Foundation — that which is taken first |
| **Heuristic** | Greek *heuriskein* | PIE *wreh₁-* | "to find" | Discovery — act of finding |
| **Maxim** | Latin *maxima* | *maximus* (superl. *magnus*) | "greatest (proposition)" | Authority — the greatest, most general premise |
| **Tenet** | Latin *tenet* | *tenere* < PIE *ten-* | "he/she holds" | Possession — a thing held as true |

## By Region

### Latin/Italic — PASS
- **principium**: Cicero uses *principia rerum* for "first principles of things"; Bentham (1789) explicitly decomposes to *primus*+*capio*
- **maxima propositio**: Boethius (c. 500 CE) coined as the warrant for topical inferences in *De topicis differentiis*; became the general term for self-evident truths
- **tenet**: originally a legal/doctrinal formula in Medieval Latin introducing a statement of belief; singular *tenet* replaced plural *tenent* (which distinguished individual vs corporate held beliefs)
- **Sources**: Perseus (Tufts), SEP (Stanford), etymonline, BYU Rhetoric

### Greek — PASS
- **heuriskein**: only term with non-Latin origin; PIE root *wreh₁- "to find"; Archimedes' εὕρηκα
- Semantic development (La Roi 2020): physical finding (Homeric) → mental perception → mental judgment
- **heuristic** entered English in 1821 as irregular formation from *heuriskein* + *-istic*
- **Sources**: LSJ (Perseus), Beekes (Brill), La Roi (Philologia Classica)

### German — PASS
- Kant makes the **maxim/principle** distinction central: *Maxime* = subjective principle (agent-relative rule); *Prinzip* = objective principle (universal law)
- *Grundsatz* = foundational proposition (lit. "ground-sentence"), used interchangeably with *Prinzip*
- Categorical imperative tests *Maximen* for universalizability
- **Sources**: Kant (Grundlegung, KpV), Potter (UNL), Pollok (USC)

### Anglophone — (synthesized from all sources)
- English borrows all four terms via Latin/French/Greek
- **principle** (14c via OF *principe*), **maxim** (15c via OF *maxime*), **tenet** (15c directly from Latin), **heuristic** (1821 via Neo-Latin)

### Axis: Certainty
```
                     tenet
    heuristic        |          principle           maxim
    (discovery,      |          (foundation,        (greatest premise,
```

### Axis: Subjectivity
```
    subjective ←—————————————→ objective
    maxim (Kant:     tenet (held    principle     heuristic
    agent's rule)    by group)     (universal)   (discovery method)
```

### Axis: Action-direction
```
    descriptive ←—————————————→ prescriptive
    heuristic    principle    tenet       maxim
    (tool)       (guide)      (belief)    (directive)
```

### Root Metaphor Comparison
- **Principle**: *first* + *take* — you take it first, build from it
- **Heuristic**: *find* — you discover it, not guaranteed optimal
- **Maxim**: *greatest* — it outranks other propositions; the biggest premise
- **Tenet**: *hold* — you grip it, possess it, maintain it as true
