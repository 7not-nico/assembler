# Genealogy of Creative Coding: A Research Manifest

This manifest maps the evolution of creative coding from early cybernetics to contemporary critical software studies. It categorizes sources not by date, but by the "mindset" of the practitioner.

## 1. The System-Designers (Cybernetics & Formalism)
*Focus: Order, Feedback, and the Mathematical Grammar of Art.*

- **Norbert Wiener**, *Cybernetics* (1948): The foundational theory of feedback loops. Every interactive installation is a descendant of this work.
- **Max Bense**: The theory of "Information Aesthetics." He treated art as a formal language that could be generated through mathematical rules.
- **Georg Nees**, *Generative Computergraphik* (1969): Established the first formal link between computer programs and visual aesthetics.
- **Philip Galanter**, *"What is Generative Art?"* (2003): Defined generative art through the lens of Complexity Theory (the balance between order and disorder).

## 2. The Cognitivists (Computational Creativity)
*Focus: The Nature of Intelligence, Agency, and Autonomy.*

- **Margaret Boden**, *"Creativity and Artificial Intelligence"* (1998): Defined the triad of creativity: Combinational, Exploratory, and Transformational.
- **Colton & Wiggins**, *"Computational Creativity: The Final Frontier?"* (2012): Created the modern benchmark for evaluating "autonomously creative" systems.
- **Harold Cohen (AARON)**: A lifelong study in the transition from "coding a tool" to "coding an autonomous artistic agent."

## 3. The Process-Performers (Live Coding & Bricolage)
*Focus: The Code as Performance, Iteration, and Act.*

- **McLean & Wiggins**, *"Bricolage Programming in the Creative Arts"* (2010): Argues that the *process* of writing code is the artwork, treating programming as a form of improvisational sketching.
- **McLean & Wiggins**, *"Live Coding Towards Computational Creativity"* (2010): Established the source code as a "performance score" in the Algorave movement.
- **teamLab**: The "Ultra-Technologist" approach. Shifting agency from the individual to an interdisciplinary collective creating "borderless" immersive systems.

### 4. The Data-Sculptors (Materialism & Scale)
*Focus: The Bit as Raw Material, Quantum Scale, and Information Density.*

- **Ryoji Ikeda**: A pioneer of "Data-Materialism." His work (e.g., *data-verse*) treats raw binary data not as information, but as a physical substance to be sculpted into sound and light.
- **Vera Molnár**: The "Machine Imaginaire." Proved that algorithmic thinking is a conceptual act that precedes the actual computer.

## 5. The Criticalists (Glitch, Failure, & Software Studies)
*Focus: The Error, the Politics of Code, and Post-Digitalism.*

- **Kim Cascone**, *"The Aesthetics of Failure"* (2000): The seminal text for **Glitch Art**. Argues that digital "failures" (clipping, quantization noise) are the most honest expressions of the digital medium.
- **Matthew Fuller / Wendy Hui Kyong Chun**: *Software Studies*. They treat software as a cultural artifact and a site of power, moving beyond the "tool" metaphor to examine how code shapes society.
- **Mark C. Marino**, *"Critical Code Studies"* (2006): Proposes the analysis of source code as a literary text, examining the "intent" and "ideology" embedded in the logic.

## 6. The Implementation Canon (Community & Frameworks)
*Focus: Accessibility, Tooling, and the Democratization of the "Creative Loop".*

- **Processing (Reas & Fry, 2001):** The most influential "on-ramp" for non-programmers. Its seminal contribution was the **visual context for learning logic**, transforming the IDE into a sketch-book.
- **openFrameworks (Lieberman, Watson, Castro):** Defined the "C++ for Artists" paradigm. Its core philosophy is **"Low-Level Access"**—providing a "glue" that wraps powerful libraries (OpenCV, OpenGL) to allow artists to manipulate the raw data of media.
- **p5.js (The Processing Foundation):** Brought the Processing philosophy to the web, emphasizing **inclusivity and accessibility** by making the "canvas" available to anyone with a browser.
- **The Book of Shaders (Gonzalez Vivo & Lowe):** The definitive guide to the **GPU-centric shift**. It transitioned the field from "CPU-loops" (drawing shapes one by one) to "Parallel Processing" (calculating every pixel simultaneously).
- **The "Coding Train" Effect (Daniel Shiffman):** Represents the shift toward **Social Learning**. Shiffman's pedagogical approach turned complex computer science concepts into an accessible, conversational medium for the global community.
