**Abstract omission — universal exclusion rules** — a cross-region research audit
confirms that 12 exclusion principles for research abstracts are universally
consistent across 4 regions (North America, Western Europe, Latin America, East
Asia) with no substantive contradictions. Region-specific differences are in
emphasis and formalization (national standards in Brazil and China; Japan's
"one sentence, one idea" convention), not in the exclusion rules themselves.
Every source from every region agrees: no citations, no figures/tables, no
absent content, no undefined acronyms, no lengthy background, no jargon, no
meta-description, no exaggeration, no self-evaluation, no examples.

Pattern: universal-exclusions → standalone-form → representative-content

Implication: abstract writing guidance can be standardized globally without
region-specific variants; cultural nuance is limited to stylistic emphasis.

8 gaps.

## 1. Fundamentals

### Definition
An abstract is a standalone, self-contained summary of a research paper that
communicates purpose, methods, findings, and conclusions. It functions as a
surrogate for the full paper in databases and search indexes.

### Core Principles (Universal)
1. **Self-contained** — intelligible without the paper (all regions)
2. **Concise** — minimum words for maximum signal (all regions)
3. **Accurate** — every claim maps 1:1 to paper content (all regions)
4. **Structured** — Background→Methods→Results→Conclusions (all regions)
5. **Informative** — delivers findings, not outline (all regions)
6. **Objective** — no evaluation, no commentary, no self-appraisal (LatAm, CN, JP)
7. **Autonomous** — no forward references to figures, tables, or bibliography
   (all regions)
8. **Keyword-rich** — optimized for database indexing (all regions)

### Region-Specific Nuances
- **North America**: explicit about no hooks/quotes, no elliptical sentences
- **Western Europe**: German universities formalize word count per degree level
- **Latin America**: explicit about no first person, no personal appreciation;
  Brazil has ABNT NBR 6028 national standard
- **East Asia**: China has GB/T 6447-2025 national standard (updated 2025);
  Japan emphasizes 一文一意 (one sentence, one idea) and bans vague expressions
