# Region: Greek — TENET (δόγμα)

## Sources
- Perseus/LSJ: δόγμα (dogma) from δοκέω "to seem, think" — opinion, belief, philosophical doctrine, public decree
- Perseus (dogma in Latin): Cicero uses dogma as "philosophic tenet"
- Stanford (Sextus Empiricus): Skeptic rejection of δόγμα (dogma) as assent to non-evident propositions
- Perseus (dogma): δόγμα = "that which seems to one" — opinion, decree, ordinance

## Key Findings
- **δόγμα** (dogma) = "that which seems to one" — from δοκέω "to think, seem, suppose"
- Greek δόγμα covers: (1) personal opinion, (2) philosophical doctrine, (3) public decree/senatus consultum
- Sextus Empiricus distinguishes two senses: (1) passive assent to appearances (Skeptic can have), (2) assent to non-evident propositions (Skeptic rejects)
- Latin dogma was borrowed directly as the translation of tenet
- Root metaphor: **seeming/opinion** — what appears true to someone, less active gripping than Latin tenet
