# Region: Anglophone — TENET

## Sources
- etymonline: tenet 15c from L "he holds"; tenent obsolete 17-18c plural
- WSU (Brians): tenant/tenet confusion — same root tenere, different grammatical persons
- Dictionary.com: tenet from L tenere "to hold"; c.1600
- LII Cornell: Totten v. United States — Tenet v. Doe (CIA espionage contract case)
- Hamilton College: "Tenet/tenant confusion" writing guide
- Language Log (UPenn): tenent vs tenet historical usage
- Wikipedia (Tenet): central principle or belief

## Key Findings
- Earliest English: early 15c, adopted from Medieval Latin formula introducing doctrine
- Grammatical confusion: **tenant** (one who holds land, from OF) vs **tenet** (belief, from L direct)
- Plural form **tenent** (they hold) was used in 17th C but obsolete by c.1725
- The singular tenet won out — the distinction between individual-held and group-held was lost
- Modern meaning: "a principle or belief held as absolute truth by someone or an organization"
- Core: passive possession — not discovered (heuristic) or first (principle) but held onto
