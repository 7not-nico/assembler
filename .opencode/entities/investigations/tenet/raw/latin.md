# Region: Latin — TENET (tenet)

## Sources
- Perseus/L&S (teneo): hold, grasp, keep, possess, maintain; PIE *ten- "to stretch"
- etymonline: tenet (n.) early 15c, from L tenet "he holds" — used in Medieval Latin to introduce doctrinal statements
- WEHD: tenet from L tenet "he holds"; tenent "they hold" (obsolete plural form)
- Noah Webster 1828: "any opinion, principle, dogma or doctrine which a person believes or maintains as true"
- Perseus (tenax): holding fast, tenacious; Horace "tenax propositi" (firm of purpose)
- L&S (dogma): philosophic tenet, doctrine; translated Gk δόγμα
- Perseus (attineo): stretch to, hold near; belong to, concern

## Key Findings
- **tenere** = "to hold" — from PIE *ten- "to stretch" (connecting notion: "cause to maintain")
- **tenet** = 3rd person singular present: "he/she/it holds"
- **tenent** = 3rd person plural "they hold" — obsolete English variant, lost by c. 1725
- Used in Medieval Latin to introduce doctrinal statements → "a thing held (to be true)"
- Distinction: tenet (belief) vs tenant (leaseholder) — same root, different grammatical forms
- Root metaphor: **possession/grip** — a proposition firmly held onto
