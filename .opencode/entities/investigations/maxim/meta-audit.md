## Regional Summaries

### Latin/Italic — PASS (14 sources)
- **Boethius** (c. 500 CE) coined *maxima propositio* for the warrant of topical inference — a self-evident proposition requiring no proof
- *Maxima* substantivized from *maxima propositio / sententia / regula*
- Legal tradition: Latin maxims as *regulae* (rulers) of legal reasoning — *pacta sunt servanda*, *de minimis non curat lex*, etc.

### Greek — PASS (7 sources)
- **γνώμη** (gnōmē) from γιγνώσκω "to know" → "judgment" → "opinion" → "maxim"
- Aristotle (Rhetoric): γνώμη = universal statement about action
- Gnomic poets (Theognis, Solon, 6th C BC): verse maxims
- Latin *maxima* translates Greek γνώμη/ἀξίωμα

### French — PASS (9 sources, 33% commercial/WARN)
- *maxime* enters French ca. 1330 (scholastic), moral sense 1485
- **La Rochefoucauld** (1665): *Réflexions ou Sentences et Maximes morales* — established maxim as literary genre
- Distinction: *réflexion* (personal) vs *sentence* (authoritative) vs *maxime* (general truth, authorless)
- Boethius's *maxima propositio* cited as logical origin (Goyet)

### German — PASS (9 sources)
- Kant: *Maxime* = subjective principle of willing (vs objective *praktisches Gesetz*)
- Categorical imperative tests maxims for universalizability
- Three-level model: i-maxims (tokens), a-maxims (abstract), k-maxims (kinds)
- *Maxime* entered German via Latin (16c) then French influence (17c)
