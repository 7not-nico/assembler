**Thunnini (tunas)** — a tribe of ray-finned pelagic fish in the family Scombridae (mackerels, bonitos, and tunas), comprising 5 genera and ~15 species. Tunas are obligate ram-ventilators (must swim continuously to oxygenate blood) and regionally endothermic (warm-bodied) — a feature unique among teleost fishes. They range from 0.5 m (bullet tuna, *Auxis rochei*) to 4.6 m (Atlantic bluefin tuna, *Thunnus thynnus*).

Seven species support major commercial fisheries: skipjack (*Katsuwonus pelamis*), yellowfin (*Thunnus albacares*), bigeye (*T. obesus*), albacore (*T. alalunga*), Atlantic bluefin (*T. thynnus*), Pacific bluefin (*T. orientalis*), and southern bluefin (*T. maccoyii*). Eight minor/neritic species (genera *Allothunnus*, *Auxis*, *Euthynnus*, *Thunnus*) sustain smaller-scale fisheries. Global catch reached ~5.8 million tonnes in 2024 (ISSF).

Tuna occupy a key trophic position in pelagic ecosystems as apex predators, and their conservation is managed by five Regional Fisheries Management Organizations (RFMOs). Several species are overfished or subject to IUU (illegal, unreported, unregulated) fishing.
