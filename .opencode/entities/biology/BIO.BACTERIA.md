**Bacteria** — a domain of prokaryotic microorganisms, typically 0.5–5 μm, lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. From Greek *bakterion* "small rod." Bacteria are the most abundant and diverse domain of life, inhabiting soil, water, symbioses, and extreme environments. They are essential to biogeochemical cycles (nitrogen fixation, decomposition) and include both pathogenic and beneficial species in relation to humans.

Bacteria are classified as Gram-positive or Gram-negative by cell wall structure, and by morphology (cocci, bacilli, spirilla, vibrios). They reproduce asexually through binary fission and exchange genetic material via conjugation, transformation, and transduction. The domain comprises two major phyla: Firmicutes and Pseudomonadota, among over 30 recognized phyla.
