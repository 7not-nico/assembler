**Tardigrada** — A phylum of microscopic segmented animals (water bears), known for extreme environmental tolerance through cryptobiosis. From Latin *tardus* "slow" + *gradus* "step" — "slow-stepper" (coined by Spallanzani, 1776). Tardigrades are found worldwide in marine, freshwater, and limno-terrestrial habitats, requiring at least a water film for active life.

Tardigrada is a physical phylum within Ecdysozoa (molting animals), sister group to Arthropoda + Onychophora. They range from 0.05–1.2 mm in length, with four pairs of unjointed legs, a bilaterally symmetric body with a complete digestive tract, and a buccal apparatus with stylets for piercing food. Tardigrades are renowned for cryptobiosis — entering an ametabolic tun state by expelling body water, allowing survival of desiccation, extreme temperatures, high pressure, radiation, and vacuum of space.
