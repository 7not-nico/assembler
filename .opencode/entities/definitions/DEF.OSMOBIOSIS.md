**Osmobiosis** — a form of cryptobiosis induced by high external osmotic pressure, in which an organism reversibly arrests its metabolism when the solute concentration in its surrounding medium exceeds a threshold. From *osmo-* (osmosis, push, impulse) + *-biosis* (mode of life) — the state of life under osmotic stress. Osmobiosis is the least studied of the four cryptobiosis forms classified by Keilin (1959), the others being anhydrobiosis (desiccation), cryobiosis (freezing), and anoxybiosis (oxygen lack).

The inducing factor is not specific to salts — any sufficiently high external osmolyte concentration increases osmotic pressure, reducing water availability. The organism responds by entering a dormant ametabolic state, often involving tun formation (in tardigrades), with metabolic activity falling below measurable levels. The state is reversible: when osmotic conditions normalize, the organism resumes active life without apparent damage.

Osmobiosis is documented primarily in marine tardigrades such as *Echiniscoides sigismundi*, which enter the tun state in saturated seawater. Research by Møbjerg et al. (2023) supports the hypothesis that osmobiosis represents the evolutionary forerunner of anhydrobiosis — the capacity to withstand salinity variation in tidal marine environments may have been the ancestral mechanism from which terrestrial desiccation tolerance evolved. Osmobiosis may also occur in nematodes, rotifers, and brine shrimp, though systematic study remains sparse.

Some authors (Wright et al., 1992) questioned whether osmobiosis constitutes "true" cryptobiosis, arguing that osmotic stress tolerance may involve energy-dependent osmoregulation rather than a fully ametabolic state. Recent evidence from tardigrade studies supports the classification as a true cryptobiotic state, with metabolism falling to near-undetectable levels.
